# Bibliographic Notes: Das Reich ohne Raum

## Publication History

### First Edition (1919)
- **Publisher**: Gustav Kiepenheuer Verlag, Potsdam
- **Print Run**: 1.-5. Tausend (copies 1-5,000)
- **Chapters**: 15 total
- **Dedication**: "An Ferruccio Busoni" (To Ferruccio Busoni) - the composer was still alive

### Third Edition (1962)
- **Publisher**: Origo Verlag, Zürich
- **Cover**: Max Kämpf
- **Chapters**: 18 total
- **Dedication**: "In Memoriam Ferruccio Busoni" (changed after Busoni's death in 1924)
- **Additions**:
  - Foreword by Walter R. Corti
  - Commentary by M. L. von Franz
  - "An Fo" poem
  - Three additional chapters in Part Two

---

## Key Discovery: The "Cut" Chapters

Marie-Louise von Franz stated in her *Problem of the Puer Aeternus* lectures that the publisher "cut out some of the chapters—I will tell you which later" when Goetz was away, causing the novel to be "misunderstood as a political pamphlet."

**However, comparison of the 1919 first edition against the 1962 third edition reveals:**

The chapters were not *removed* from the first edition—they were *added* to later editions. Specifically:

### Part Two: Die Wanderung (The Wandering)

| 1919 First Edition | 1962 Third Edition |
|---|---|
| Chapter 7: The Open Gate | Chapter 7: The Open Gate |
| *(Part Two ends)* | Chapter 8: The Judgment |
| | Chapter 9: The Call |
| | Chapter 10: The Crucifixion and the Animals |

**Result**: Part Two originally had only ONE chapter. The three mystical "wandering" chapters (The Judgment, The Call, The Crucifixion and the Animals) were added in later editions.

### Why the Book Was "Misunderstood as Political"

Without the mystical wandering chapters (8-10), the narrative jumps directly from Melchior's departure through "The Open Gate" to the politically-charged Stuhlbrestenburg chapters (the uprising, the trial, the chaos). The original structure:

1. Part One: Mystical awakening in Schimmelberg
2. Part Two: Departure (1 chapter only)
3. Part Three: Political satire in Stuhlbrestenburg
4. Part Four: Mystical resolution

The expanded 1962 version adds three chapters of otherworldly experience between Parts One and Three, balancing the political content with more explicitly mystical material.

## Front Matter Differences

### 1919 First Edition
- Title page only
- "An Ferruccio Busoni" dedication poem (14 lines, formal sonnet structure)
- No foreword
- No "An Fo" poem

### 1962 Third Edition
- Title page with commentary credit
- Foreword by Walter R. Corti
- "In Memoriam Ferruccio Busoni" (same poem, changed title)
- "An Fo" poem (7 stanzas)
- Von Franz commentary

## Von Franz and the First Edition

In her lectures, von Franz stated:

> "I have not been able to find the first [edition]"

She worked from the 1962 third edition. Her statement about "cut chapters" appears to be a misunderstanding—either:
1. Goetz told her chapters were cut (when in fact they were added later)
2. She assumed the expanded version was original
3. There is an unknown second edition with different content

**Significance**: The owner of this translation project possesses a 1919 first edition that von Franz herself could not locate.
