## TO FERRUCCIO BUSONI

Into smoke and rubble sank what we had known,
encircled by death in dark gigantic arc.
After the dream that flew before us,
our spirit presses on in tempestuous fire.

Far from homeland and motherly land
our ship drifts on uncertain waves.
Laughing boldly we set out—
seafarers we toward undiscovered land.

And when night and horror overcome us,
you sing us free songs of a new homeland—
and all specters float away, a light down.

In rhythm and round the world dissolves brightly,
from stars late happiness trickles down,
glowing radiant shines for us the Kingdom without Space.

**NOTE: This is a structural template showing the 1919 First Edition chapter organization.**

**Key differences from the 1962 Third Edition:**

1. **Title page**: Gustav Kiepenheuer Verlag Potsdam, not Origo Verlag Zürich
2. **Dedication poem**: "AN FERRUCCIO BUSONI" (To Ferruccio Busoni) - Busoni was still alive in 1919
3. **No "AN FO" poem** - This poem was added in later editions
4. **No FOREWORD** - Walter Corti's foreword was added for the 1962 edition
5. **Part Two has only ONE chapter** (Chapter 7: The Open Gate)
   - Chapters 8-10 of the third edition (The Judgment, The Call, The Crucifixion and the Animals) were added later
6. **15 total chapters** instead of 18

The full translation text for each chapter can be found in the main "Das Reich ohne Raum -- English.md" file. This template documents the original 1919 structure.
