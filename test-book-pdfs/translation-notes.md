# Das Reich ohne Raum — Translation Notes

A record of translation choices, comparing the German original with von Franz's English retelling, and documenting decisions made for the literal translation.

---

## Dedication Poems

### IN MEMORIAM FERRUCCIO BUSONI

| German | Literal Translation | von Franz | Notes |
|--------|---------------------|-----------|-------|
| *In Rauch und Trümmer sank* | Into smoke and rubble sank | In smoke and ruins sank | "Trümmer" = rubble/debris, more specific than "ruins" |
| *vom Tod umkreist in dunklem Riesenbogen* | encircled by death in dark gigantic arc | — (omitted) | von Franz loses this powerful image entirely |
| *in ungestümem Brand* | in tempestuous fire | — | "Brand" = fire/blaze, not just metaphorical |
| *Seefahrer wir* | seafarers we | — | von Franz added "Vikings" which is NOT in the original |
| *aufglüht strahlt das Reich uns ohne Raum* | glowing radiant shines for us the Kingdom without Space | — | Preserved the unusual word order |

**Structural note**: This is a Petrarchan sonnet (ABBA ABBA CDC DCD). von Franz collapsed the formal structure.

## Chapter One: Schimmelberg

### Key Translation Choices

| German | Literal | von Franz | Decision |
|--------|---------|-----------|----------|
| *Wetterkragen* | storm collars | weather collars | "Storm" captures the dramatic quality |
| *prallte betroffen zurück* | recoiled in dismay | recoiled as if in surprise | "betroffen" = dismayed/struck, stronger than surprise |
| *zerfurchten Gesicht* | furrowed face | lean, furrowed face | von Franz adds "lean" — not in original |
| *wunderlichen Sohn* | peculiar son | strange son | "wunderlich" has connotations of wonder/strangeness |
| *knabenhaften Vorwitz* | boyish presumption | — (omitted) | von Franz skips this characterization |
| *verbissener Nüchternheit* | dogged sobriety | down-to-earth, cynical | Literal is more vivid |
| *Herzschlag* | heart attack | died peacefully | von Franz interprets; Goetz states fact |

### Stylistic Observations

- von Franz *summarizes* and *interprets*; Goetz's prose is more matter-of-fact
- The German lets strangeness speak for itself without psychological framing
- Goetz's narrator is detached, almost journalistic in the opening

## Chapter Two: The Encounter

### Key Translation Choices

| German | Literal | von Franz | Decision |
|--------|---------|-----------|----------|
| *immer dichter fallenden Regen* | ever more densely falling rain | heavy rain falling | Literal preserves the progressive quality |
| *hielt sich vorsichtig am Wegesrand* | kept carefully to the path's edge | disappeared | von Franz has him vanish immediately — Goetz shows tactical movement |
| *wechselte seinen Ausdruck von Sekunde zu Sekunde* | changed expression from second to second | seemed to change in expression | Goetz is more definite |
| *mächtige Stirn von strahlendem Weiß* | mighty brow of radiant white | very large forehead | "mächtig" and "strahlend" are more dramatic |
| *beklemmender Freude* | oppressive joy | tremendous happiness | Oxymoron preserved — happiness tinged with dread |
| *durchfuhr es heiß* | a wave of heat shot through | felt tremendously warm sympathy | Literal is more visceral, less interpretive |

### Stylistic Observations

- The chapter has cinematic quality — we watch von Spät's face shifting like a film
- von Franz flattens the prose; Goetz's German has more physical/sensory detail
- The handshake scene is pivotal: Melchior's betrayal is immediate and physical

- Fo's departure: flame + sparks = the boys are elemental, not just supernatural

- The naked women, the pompous speeches about "Art" and "Spirit" — savage irony
- Trümpelsteg's speech is a masterpiece of self-deluding rhetoric
- Melchior and von Spät observe with detached amusement — they're the only real people in the room

| *stob von dannen* | shot away | — | Fo's explosive departure |

### The Old Woman's Prophecy

The huckster-woman delivers a **poem** — rhythm and line breaks preserved:

| German | Literal |
|--------|---------|
| *Ring am Finger,* | Ring on finger, |
| *Gesichter am Fenster.* | Faces at the window. |
| *Wege kreuzen sich.* | Paths cross. |
| *Winde wehn südwärts.* | Winds blow southward. |
| *Bald ist es Zeit.* | Soon it is time. |
| *Sie warten. Sie warten.* | They wait. They wait. |

### Stylistic Observations

- This chapter is **magical realism at its most intense** — von Spät's blood magic, the sevenfold multiplication
- Professor Schulze's speech is **bitter irony** — rationalism swallowing the miraculous whole
- Fo's triumph is **visceral and frightening** — his body glows, his hair becomes "dark flames" (*Seine Haare waren dunkle Flammen*)
- The huckster-woman reappears as magical servant — same figure from Chapter 3, now serving von Spät
- The imprisoned lovers (Trümpelsteg and Frau Cux) — von Franz calls this the intellect reducing love to "animal 'nothing-but'"
- Melchior's ambivalence: he wants to stop Fo from tormenting von Spät — "Horror and love overwhelmed him"
- Fo's final words: *"Melchior, wir warten auf dich!"* — the call that will echo through the novel

## Chapter Six: Before the Decision

**Note**: von Franz DOES narrate portions of this chapter (the ultimatum scene). This allows for direct comparison.

### Key Translation Choices

| German | Literal | von Franz | Decision |
|--------|---------|-----------|----------|
| *herrlichem Ebenmaß* | splendid symmetry | — | Von Spät's body described as perfect |
| *Antlitz eines schönen Götterbildes* | countenance of a beautiful god-image | — | "Götterbild" = god-image/idol |
| *ins Längliche, ja Kranke verzogen* | distorted toward the elongated, indeed the sickly | — | The sickness in beauty |
| *Widerspiel der Sternenwelt* | counter-play of the starry world | — | Von Spät's cosmic doctrine |
| *ich will mich verschenken* | I want to give myself away | — | Melchior's earthly counter |
| *zügellosen Freien* | unbridled free ones | wild boys | "unbridled" keeps horse metaphor |
| *wahllose wilde Liebe* | indiscriminate wild love | wild love | "wahllos" = random/indiscriminate |
| *Quell des Schlafs* | wellspring of sleep | source of sleep | "Quell" = spring/fountain |
| *süße Nacht* | sweet night | sweet darkness | "Nacht" = night, not "darkness" |
| *Herrschaft im Licht* | mastery in light | ruling of the light | "Herrschaft" = mastery/dominion |
| *entführen* | carry off | seduce | "entführen" = abduct, not seduce |

### The Ultimatum

Von Spät presents the binary choice that structures the rest of the novel:

| Choice | German | Action | Result |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Fo's way** | *süße Nacht* | Call out | Forget who you were and are |
| **Von Spät's way** | *Herrschaft im Licht* | Knock on wall | Path through horror to mastery |

### Stylistic Observations

- Von Spät's naked body is described with **homoerotic reverence** — "splendid symmetry," "most delicate softness"
- The "beautiful god-image" distorted "toward the sickly" — beauty and illness intertwined
- Melchior calls von Spät "**my brother**" — recognition of kinship despite conflict
- Von Spät's doctrine: "Our body is not earth. Our body is music" — **Gnostic rejection of matter**
- Melchior's counter: "I love the earth. I want to give myself away!" — the **puer's earthly longing**
- The ceiling opens to reveal von Spät's **double/mirror-image** — cosmic hierarchy implied
- Von Spät's voice becomes "the distant ringing of glass bells" — disembodied, crystalline
- Chapter ends with Melchior **alone** — the choice not yet made, Part One complete

### End of Part One

This chapter concludes **Part One: The Message** (*Die Botschaft*). The six chapters have established:
1. Melchior's backstory and alienation (Ch. 1)
2. The encounter with Fo and von Spät (Ch. 2)
3. The ring and the transformed city (Ch. 3)
4. The bourgeois salon grotesque (Ch. 4)
5. Von Spät's magic and Fo's triumph (Ch. 5)
6. The ultimatum: choose (Ch. 6)

Part Two: **The Wandering** (*Die Wanderung*) begins with Melchior's choice.

## Part Two: The Wandering

### Chapter Seven: The Open Gate

**Note**: Von Franz briefly narrates this chapter. She interprets Melchior's action as choosing von Spät's way.

#### Key Translation Choices

| German | Literal | von Franz | Decision |
|--------|---------|-----------|----------|
| *grübelte* | was brooding | — | Dark pondering |
| *hellerem, leichterem Licht* | brighter, lighter light | — | Alliteration preserved |
| *Ich muß Gewißheit haben!* | I must have certainty! | — | The moment of choice |
| *grünblauer Nacht* | green-blue night | — | Supernatural sea color |
| *Böse tote Stille* | evil dead silence | — | "Böse" = evil |
| *uferlos nächtiges Meer* | shoreless nocturnal sea | — | "Nächtiges" = belonging to night |
| *Vermummte* | muffled ones | muffled figures | Consistent throughout |
| *Flammenschrift* | flame-writing | — | Goetz's compound |
| *Überfülle von Licht* | superabundance of light | — | Courtroom revelation |

#### Critical Note on the Choice

Von Franz says Melchior "chooses von Spät's way" by knocking on the wall. But Goetz writes that Melchior **strikes** (*schlug mit den Fäusten*) the wall, not **knocks** (*poche*) as von Spät instructed. This may be significant — an impulsive blow vs. a deliberate knock.

#### Imagery

- **Gate to sea**: Water initiation journey
- **Soundless sailing ship**: Death-ship / underworld ferry
- **Flame-writing**: New alphabet between worlds
- **Bound and blinded**: Initiatory ordeal
- **Red velvet hall with three judges**: Soul tribunal
- **Everyone from Melchior's life**: Total judgment

### Chapter Nine: The Call

**Note**: Von Franz covers this chapter extensively — wolves (devouring mother), mushroom marketplace (morbid nature), crystal palace (von Spät's kingdom), and Melchior's cry "I want to leave!"

#### Key Translation Choices

| German | Literal | von Franz | Decision |

| *Ausverkauf!* | Clearance sale! | clearance sale | Kept exclamation — captures marketplace frenzy |
| *Schwammerlmarkt* | mushroom market | — | "mushroom marketplace" — Austrian dialect |
| *Hutpilz* | hat-mushroom | — | "cap-mushroom" — describes umbrella shape |
| *grünfarbenen Gletschern* | green-colored glaciers | green glaciers | "green glaciers" — surreal color preserved |
| *Glasherren* | glass lords | lords of glass | "glass lords" — compound preserved |
| *blausteinernen Augen* | blue-stone eyes | blue-stony eyes | "eyes of blue stone" — mineral quality |
| *Kronenträger* | crown-bearers | crown-bearers | Same — frozen boys' role |
| *erstarrte Knaben* | frozen/rigid boys | frozen boys | "frozen boys" — *erstarrt* = stiffened |
| *Willst du nicht fort von hier?* | Do you not want to leave here? | Do you want to leave? | Kept negative — more plaintive |
| *Johanniswürmchen* | little St. John's worms | fireflies | "fireflies" — German folk name |

#### Von Franz's Psychological Commentary

Von Franz interprets this chapter extensively:
- **Wolves**: Symbol of the devouring mother; also Germanic death god with wolf's head
- **Mushroom marketplace**: Morbid, decaying nature; connects to drugs undermining moral effort
- **Crystal palace**: Von Spät's kingdom of rigid reason without life
- **Frozen boys**: Fo's band captured — life petrified by intellect
- **"I want to leave!"**: Enantiodromia — pendulum swings back to Fo

#### Stylistic Observations

- **Austrian dialect**: *Schwammerlmarkt* uses Bavarian/Austrian *Schwammerl* for mushroom
- **Compound words**: *Glasherren*, *blausteinernen*, *Kronenträger* — preserved
- **Sensory overload**: The mushroom marketplace is almost hallucinogenic — fermentation, yellow dust, violet-red flesh
- **Naked huckster-woman**: Same figure from earlier chapters, now grotesquely sexualized
- **Fo's eyes in crown-bearers**: Even in the glass prison, Fo is present

### Chapter Ten: The Crucifixion and the Animals

**Note**: Von Franz covers this chapter extensively — Fo as moon-god, the crucifixion as initiation, the World Tree transformation, and the naming of "Li."

#### Key Translation Choices

| German | Literal | von Franz | Decision |
|--------|---------|-----------|----------|
| *bleicher Strom* | pale stream | water, not blood | "pale stream" — *bleich* = pale/white |
| *tönende Wellen* | sounding waves | waves of sound | "sounding waves" — Fo becomes pure music |
| *unförmig* | monstrously | — | Moon swells grotesquely before descending |
| *Kreuziget, kreuziget ihn!* | Crucify, crucify him! | — | Gospel echo but **jubilant**, not hostile |
| *Dornenkranz aus Licht* | crown of thorns of light | crown of thorns | Crown is **woven of light** — crucial |
| *kalte Schatten* | cold shadows | cold shadows | Nails feel insubstantial |
| *nächtigen Grund* | nocturnal ground | — | Roots drink from night/darkness |
| *feuchte Glut* | moist fire | — | Oxymoron: living earth substance |
| *Weidenrohr* | willow-reed | flute | Specific Pan-like imagery |
| *gefriedet* | at peace | — | Archaic: animals "peaced" |
| *Alle Tiere kehren heim* | All animals return home | All animals return | Added "home" — *heim* is crucial |
| *Li* | Li | Li | Chinese: consciousness/reason/pattern |

#### Von Franz's Psychological Commentary

Von Franz provides extensive mythological amplification:
- **Fo as moon-god**: Speared, white water (not blood) flows, dissolves into sound, merges with moon, returns
- **Dionysus/Attis parallel**: God dies in spring, resurrects — Fo embodies archaic vegetation deity
- **World Tree (Yggdrasil)**: Melchior becomes the tree — roots in darkness, branches in light, springs flowing
- **Li = Chinese concept**: Von Franz notes Li (理) means "reason" or the secret patterns in precious stones — Melchior as ego-consciousness
- **Fo's unanswered question**: "And why must we wander?" — Fo deflects, glorifies movement without goal

#### Stylistic Observations

- **White blood**: Fo bleeds water/light, not red blood — elemental rather than mortal
- **Jubilant crucifixion**: "Crucify him!" is a cry of **joy** — initiation, not execution
- **Transformation sequence**: Cross → tree → World Tree → boy — dissolution of boundaries
- **Animal gathering**: Lions and lambs together — Isaianic vision of peaceable kingdom
- **"Li! Li! Li!"**: The naming ceremony — triple invocation like ritual formula

This chapter concludes **Part Two: The Wandering** (*Die Wanderung*). Melchior has:

2. Faced judgment and escaped (Ch. 8)
3. Passed through wolves, marketplace, crystal palace (Ch. 9)
4. Been initiated into Fo's band through crucifixion (Ch. 10)

### Chapter Eleven: Stuhlbrestenburg

**Note**: Von Franz provides extensive commentary — the architecture as symbol of Germany's psyche, the chaos as prophetic of actual historical events.

#### Key Translation Choices

| German | Literal | von Franz | Decision |
|--------|---------|-----------|----------|
| *Stuhlbrestenburg* | Stool-Disease-Castle | Excrement-Disease-Town | Kept German — von Franz decodes it |
| *Rattenhusen* | Rat-Houses | Rattenhausen | Kept German — "Rat-dwelling" |

| *Sigillum signum veri* | The seal is the sign of truth | — | Kept Latin — pompous officialese |

#### Von Franz's Psychological Commentary

Von Franz sees this chapter as deeply prophetic:
- **Burned lower levels + Rococo upper stories**: Germany's split psyche — archaic depths covered by thin civilization
- **"Subterraneans"**: The repressed shadow, criminal element
- **Mass psychosis**: Von Franz wrote after WWII, seeing the pattern repeat
- **The newspaper's absurd explanations**: Satire of rationalist denial

#### Stylistic Observations

- **Radical shift in style**: From mystical visions to social satire
- **Satirical targets**: Bureaucrats, newspapers, pompous doctors
- **"Sherlock Holmes, Karl Marx, Alexandre Dumas"**: The newspaper blames reading for irrationality
- **Cold foot-baths**: The psychiatrist's advice is comically inadequate

## Translation Principles (Summary)

1. **Literal over interpretive**: Preserve Goetz's word choices, even when strange
2. **Physical/sensory detail**: Keep the visceral language von Franz smooths over
3. **Archaic vocabulary**: Use slightly archaic English for archaic German (Hökerfrau → huckster-woman)
4. **Compound words**: Preserve Goetz's German compounds (Athermeer → ether-sea)
5. **Sentence structure**: Where natural, keep Goetz's unusual word order
6. **No psychological framing**: Let the strangeness speak for itself
7. **Preserve irony**: Goetz is often satirical — don't flatten it
