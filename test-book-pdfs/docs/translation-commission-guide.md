# Commissioning a Professional Translation of *Das Reich ohne Raum*

A guide to getting Bruno Goetz's 1919 German mystical novel professionally translated into English.

---

## The Opportunity

**No published English translation of this book exists.** The only English renderings are Marie-Louise von Franz's summaries in her Jungian lectures on the *Puer Aeternus*. A professional translation would be the **first ever**.

---

## About the Book

| Detail | Information |
|--------|-------------|
| **Title** | *Das Reich ohne Raum* (The Kingdom Without Space) |
| **Author** | Bruno Goetz (1885–1954) |
| **First Published** | 1919, Kiepenheuer, Potsdam |
| **Genre** | German symbolist/expressionist literary fiction |
| **Comparable Authors** | Hermann Hesse, Gustav Meyrink, Franz Kafka |
| **Significance** | Analyzed by C.G. Jung in seminars; central text in von Franz's *Problem of the Puer Aeternus* |

### Editions

| Edition | Year | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|
| 1st Edition | 1919 | Kiepenheuer, Potsdam. 159 pages. Later described as "incomplete" |
| 2nd+ Edition | 1925 | Added subtitle "Eine Chronik wunderlicher Begebenheiten" (A Chronicle of Wondrous Events). This is the **complete** version |
| Later Edition | 1995 | Includes commentary by Marie-Louise von Franz |

**Important:** The 1925 edition explicitly states that "an incomplete edition had already appeared in 1919."

## Copyright Status

**Public Domain as of January 1, 2025**

- Bruno Goetz died March 19, 1954
- German/EU copyright law: life + 70 years
- 70 years elapsed on January 1, 2025
- **No permission needed** from heirs or estate to translate and publish

This means:
- Anyone can translate and publish freely
- Your translation itself would be copyrightable as a new derivative work
- No rights acquisition costs

## How Literary Translation Commissioning Works

### The Normal Ecosystem

Literary translation usually flows:
1. Publisher buys foreign rights → Publisher hires translator → Book published
2. Translator loves a book → Translator applies for grant → Translator pitches to publishers

**Private patronage** (individual paying for a translation) is unusual but not unheard of.

### What Translators Want

Literary translators typically want three things:
1. **Their name on the cover** (in same font as author)
2. **Copyright to the translation** in their name
3. **Royalties** if the book sells

### Arrangement Options

| Arrangement | Translator Gets | You Get |
|-------------|-----------------|---------|
| **Work-for-hire** | Flat fee only | Full copyright ownership, publishing rights |
| **Patronage/Sponsorship** | Copyright + credit | Acknowledgment as patron/sponsor |
| **Co-publishing** | Copyright + credit + royalties | Publishing rights, shared royalties, "edited by" or "project by" credit |

**Note:** Many literary translators refuse work-for-hire for literary projects—they see their work as creative authorship, not commodity production.

### Estimated Costs

- Literary translators typically charge **$0.08–0.12 per word**
- For a ~40,000–50,000 word novel: **$3,200–$6,000**
- Quality translation takes 60–100+ hours minimum

## Recommended Approach

### Option A: Contact Mike Mitchell / Dedalus Books Directly

This is the most promising path given:
- His expertise in exactly this literary era and style
- Dedalus's specialty in German decadent/expressionist literature
- Possibility they'd want to publish it (with you as sponsor)

### Sample Inquiry Email

> **Subject:** Commissioning first English translation of Bruno Goetz's *Das Reich ohne Raum*
>
> Dear [Mike Mitchell / Dedalus Books],
>
> I'm interested in commissioning the first English translation of Bruno Goetz's *Das Reich ohne Raum* (1919/1925)—a German symbolist novel analyzed extensively by Marie-Louise von Franz in her Jungian lectures on the *Puer Aeternus*.
>
> The book entered public domain in January 2025, and to my knowledge no English translation has ever been published. Given your work on Meyrink and early 20th-century German literature, I wondered if you might be interested in discussing this project.
>
> I'm open to various arrangements—sponsorship, co-publishing, or other structures that would make sense for a project like this.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your name]

### Option B: Find a Translator via Reedsy

- Visit https://reedsy.com/translation/book-translator
- Filter by: **Literary Fiction**, German to English
- In your request, specify: "early 20th century German prose, symbolist/expressionist style, dream-like narrative"

### Option C: Kickstarter Campaign

- Partner with a translator willing to commit if funded
- Pitch: "Help bring this lost German mystical novel to English for the first time"
- The Jungian/von Franz angle could attract niche backers
